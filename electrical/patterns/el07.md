# EL07 — Protective device type from a product description

[Electrical catalog]({{ '/electrical/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/electrical/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/electrical.md)

Classify which protective device type a product description denotes.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/electrical/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Catches a submittal or purchase that describes the wrong protection type before it is installed; ratings and listings stay in code.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `product_description`
- `required_type`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 5 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.84; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `EL07-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "product_description": "Tamper-resistant, weather-resistant 20 A / 125 V duplex receptacle with built-in surge protection and a green protection-status indicator light that goes out when surge protection is no longer active. Self-grounding mounting strap, back-wire and side-wire terminals, and an optional audible alarm. Rated for use with wet-location in-use covers; nylon face in white, ivory, or gray.",
  "required_type": "gfci"
}
```

Expected label: `standard`.

Surge protection, tamper and weather resistance, a protection-status indicator, and a self-grounding strap are none of them ground-fault protection for people or arc-fault protection, so the contract's 'neither' case gives standard; the mismatch with required_type is left to code.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code compares the label with `required_type`, checks ampere, voltage, and listing marks exactly, and holds the purchase when they differ.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; a mismatch with `required_type` goes to the estimator.

## Verification recipe

Run product listings with known types through the check; measure misclassification per manufacturer wording.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [Wikipedia: Residual-current device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residual-current_device) (ES-3). Describes ground-fault protective devices such as GFCIs as distinct from overcurrent devices. Motivates EL07 as a device-type classification; encyclopedic overview.
- [Wikipedia: Arc-fault circuit interrupter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc-fault_circuit_interrupter) (ES-5). Describes arc-fault protection and dual-function devices combining it with ground-fault protection. Motivates EL07.
- [NFPA 70, National Electrical Code (NFPA overview page)](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70) (ES-2). NFPA describes NFPA 70, the National Electrical Code, as enforced in all 50 states and the benchmark for safe electrical design, installation, and inspection to protect people and property from electrical hazards, with edition, amendment, and errata records. Motivates EL01, EL03, EL04, EL07; no article is cited and no rule is applied by the model.
- [OSHA: Electrical - Overview](https://www.osha.gov/electrical) (ES-6). OSHA's electrical overview states that electricity is a serious workplace hazard, that its standards protect employees from shock, electrocution, fires, and explosions, and that the most frequent causes of electrical injuries include contact with power lines, lack of ground-fault protection, a missing or discontinuous path to ground, equipment not used as prescribed, and improper use of extension and flexible cords. Motivates EL02 (hazard signs) and EL07 (ground-fault protection devices); the on-site electrician's determination governs.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
