# Inspiration, not a performance claim

The design pass read these public pages. Each summary describes the design need, not Jev's effectiveness. No source is represented as endorsing this catalog; all contracts and scenarios are task-authored adaptations. The SHA-256 binds the page text as fetched on the listed date, not a claim that the page is unchanged since.

## LC-S1: Wikipedia: Indemnity

[Read the source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indemnity) · checked 2026-09-23 · SHA-256 `7291fc413432d9ff…`

Describes an indemnity as one party's contractual obligation to compensate another for loss, usually paired with hold-harmless wording, and quotes clause examples in which a named party shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the other. Motivates LC01 (indemnity as a clause type), LC02 (which named party is bound), and LC03 (one-way drafting). Motivation for the questions, not evidence of Jev performance.

## LC-S2: Wikipedia: Non-disclosure agreement

[Read the source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-disclosure_agreement) · checked 2026-09-23 · SHA-256 `f0b44e7296804136…`

Classifies confidentiality agreements as unilateral (one-way), bilateral (mutual), or multilateral, and lists typical contents: the definition of what is confidential, the exclusions from what must be kept confidential, and a duty to give the disclosing party prompt notice of compelled disclosure. Motivates LC01 (confidentiality as a clause type), LC03 (reciprocal versus one-sided), LC06 (a defined term and its uses), LC07 (notice elements), and LC08 (carve-outs from a rule). Motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.

Used by: [LC01]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc01/' | relative_url }}), [LC03]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc03/' | relative_url }}), [LC06]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc06/' | relative_url }}), [LC07]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc07/' | relative_url }}), [LC08]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc08/' | relative_url }})

## LC-S3: Wikipedia: Termination for convenience

[Read the source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termination_for_convenience) · checked 2026-09-23 · SHA-256 `1602c8663cb79d07…`

Describes a termination for convenience clause as one that lets a party end a contract without establishing the other party's default, and notes standard forms that allow termination at any time by written notice. Motivates LC04 (what a term expression describes) and LC07 (whether a notice provision states its method and recipient). Motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.

Used by: [LC04]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc04/' | relative_url }}), [LC07]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc07/' | relative_url }})

## LC-S4: Wikipedia: Document comparison

[Read the source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Document_comparison) · checked 2026-09-23 · SHA-256 `554c88d8aa3229e6…`

Describes document comparison, also called redlining, as identifying changes between two versions of a document, with a section on its use in the legal industry and the convention of showing deletions and insertions in a marked-up comparison. Motivates LC05 (sorting the comments that accompany a redline by whether they seek a substantive or an editorial change). Motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.

Used by: [LC05]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc05/' | relative_url }})

## LC-S5: Wikipedia: Exclusion clause

[Read the source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusion_clause) · checked 2026-09-23 · SHA-256 `08ca1a240f47f5ae…`

Describes clauses that exclude or limit a party's liability and notes that courts read such clauses strictly and against the party relying on them. Motivates LC01 (limitation of liability as a clause type) and LC08 (whether a clause states exceptions to its own rule). How any such clause is enforced is a legal question that stays with the lawyer. Motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.

Used by: [LC01]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc01/' | relative_url }}), [LC08]({{ '/legal-contracts/patterns/lc08/' | relative_url }})
