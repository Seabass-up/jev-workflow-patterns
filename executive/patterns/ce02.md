# CE02 — Proposal describes a reversible or one-way commitment

[Executive catalog]({{ '/executive/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/executive/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/executive.md)

Classify what a proposal says about whether the commitment it asks for could be undone.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/executive/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

<div class="callout warning"><p><strong>Provisional.</strong> At least one screening case disagreed with its authored label or did not complete. The disagreement is preserved on the evaluation page; neither the contract nor the label was changed after inference.</p></div>

## Proposed benefit

Flags proposals that ask for a binding commitment without saying so, and pilots that state their own exit, so scrutiny can match what is written.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `proposal_text`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 4 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.85; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `CE02-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "proposal_text": "Proposal: enter a supply agreement with Ferrowick Metals for the Marrow product line. Ferrowick has offered us first position on its new alloy capacity, and the offer stands only until the end of the quarter. Waiting is irreversible: if we pass, Ferrowick will allocate that capacity to Corvane Industrial and there will be no second chance to recover our position in the segment. The agreement would cover pricing, volume commitments, and delivery windows, with terms to be finalized by legal. I recommend we sign."
}
```

Expected label: `reversibility_not_addressed`.

The only irreversibility language describes the consequence of not acting, not the commitment being asked for; the text never states a way to exit the agreement nor that the agreement is permanent or binding, so it says nothing about undoing the commitment itself.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code maps the label to a review track and records it beside the proposal; whether the action is in fact reversible, and how much scrutiny it deserves, is the executive team's judgment.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

One re-check after the author adds a reversibility statement to a proposal labeled reversibility_not_addressed.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with the executive team's own reading of reversibility on past proposals; review first every binding commitment the label called reversible or unaddressed.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [Wikipedia: Real options valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_options_valuation) (CE-S4). Describes management flexibility to defer, abandon, expand, stage, or contract a project as valuable under uncertainty, in contrast with irreversible investment. Motivates CE02 as a reading of whether a proposal describes its own commitment as reversible or one-way; the model performs no valuation. This is motivation for the question, not evidence of Jev performance.
- [Wikipedia: Business case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_case) (CE-S2). Describes a formal business case as including background, expected benefits, the options considered with reasons for rejecting or carrying forward each, expected costs and risks, and consideration of doing nothing, and lists measurable key aspects among the marks of a good case. Motivates CE04, CE06, and CE07 as completeness readings of proposal text. This is motivation for the questions, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
