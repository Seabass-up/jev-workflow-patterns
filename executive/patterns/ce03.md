# CE03 — Evidence offered is data, anecdote, or none

[Executive catalog]({{ '/executive/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/executive/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/executive.md)

Classify whether a proposal supports its recommendation with systematic quantitative evidence, one or a few examples, or no evidence.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/executive/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Distinguishes evidence gathered across cases from an anecdote that may include numbers, and from unsupported assertion, without judging whether the support is sufficient.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `proposal_text`

Consumer code must deterministically return `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; this collection does not implement or verify that gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

  "decision": {
    "type": "choice",
    "instructions": "First, if any required field (`proposal_text`) is absent, empty, or lacks the information needed for this judgment, select unknown before considering other labels. Classify the strongest kind of evidence that `proposal_text` offers in support of its recommendation; do not count numbers that are merely incidental to an example or budget. Quantitative data means counts, measurements, surveys, financial analyses, or test results gathered across cases or otherwise presented as systematic evidence for the recommendation. A one-off measurement or number embedded in one or a few specific examples, such as a named customer's experience, a colleague's report, or the author's own observation, remains anecdote_or_example. A requested budget or cost, by itself, is not evidence for the claimed benefit or effect. A recommendation stated without any instance, report, or systematic figure in support, including appeals to what is obvious or what everyone knows, offers no evidence. ## Complete synthetic example

This is reviewer-authored regression fixture `CE03-5`, added after an earlier boundary finding to test the revised contract. Its expected label was recorded before the current screen; it is not an independent or held-out case.

```json
{
  "proposal_text": "Recommendation: add a short equipment check at the start of each shift. Last Friday at the Harrow plant I timed one panel check at exactly 18 minutes, and two technicians told me this was typical of their work. We should reserve a 20-minute slot. The operations appendix has not yet aggregated any further observations."
}
```

Expected label: `anecdote_or_example`.

The numeric time and technician count are details from one observed situation and informal reports; the proposal states that no aggregate or systematic evidence is available.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code attaches the label to the proposal summary and returns no_evidence_offered proposals to the author; the accuracy, source, and sufficiency of any figure are checked by people.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; a proposal labeled no_evidence_offered goes back to the author, not to another model call.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with an analyst's classification of the evidence in a set of past proposals; review disagreements between anecdote_or_example and quantitative_data first.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

Describes anecdotal evidence as personal experiences, self-reported claims, and eyewitness accounts collected non-systematically, and notes that one or a few anecdotes risk being unrepresentative. Motivates CE03 as a classification of the kind of evidence a proposal offers, distinct from whether the evidence is correct. This is motivation for the question, not evidence of Jev performance.
- [Wikipedia: Business case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_case) (CE-S2). Describes a formal business case as including background, expected benefits, the options considered with reasons for rejecting or carrying forward each, expected costs and risks, and consideration of doing nothing, and lists measurable key aspects among the marks of a good case. Motivates CE04, CE06, and CE07 as completeness readings of proposal text. This is motivation for the questions, not evidence of Jev performance.
