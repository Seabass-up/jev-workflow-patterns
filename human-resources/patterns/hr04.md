# HR04 — Route a reported concern by the organization's intake policy

[Human resources catalog]({{ '/human-resources/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/human-resources/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/human-resources.md)

Classify which intake route a reported workplace concern belongs to, using the organization's own policy text as the definition of each route.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/human-resources/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Gets a concern to the process that handles it without a person re-reading every intake message, while the supplied policy text defines the routes and people handle the concern.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `concern_text`
- `policy_routes`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 5 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.84; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `HR04-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "concern_text": "The lights in stairwell B on the north side have been out for two weeks. I emailed facilities twice and have not heard back. That stairwell is the only way down to the loading dock after the elevator locks at 6pm, so people are using their phones to see the steps.",
  "policy_routes": {
    "safety": "Physical hazards, injuries, unsafe equipment or conditions, and any lighting failure in a stairwell, parking area, or loading dock.",
    "employee_relations": "How an employee was treated by a coworker or manager, including disputes, communication, and team conflict.",
    "ethics_compliance": "Suspected fraud, theft, falsified records, conflicts of interest, and misuse of company funds or data.",
    "workplace_services": "Building maintenance, access badges, broken tools, and missing supplies that pose no hazard, including follow-up on unanswered facilities tickets."
  }
}
```

Expected label: `safety`.

The concern reads like an unanswered facilities ticket, but the supplied policy text explicitly assigns stairwell lighting failures to the safety route, and the contract routes by policy_routes with safety first in precedence when more than one route fits.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code keeps the intake policy text and its revision, opens the case in the route's system, records who receives it, and applies the standing rule that any concern describing imminent physical danger is escalated immediately regardless of label. People investigate and decide.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; unknown concerns go to the intake coordinator, never back to the reporter for rewording.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with the route an intake coordinator chose for past concerns; review every concern the label sent away from safety or employee_relations first.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission: Prohibited Employment Policies/Practices](https://www.eeoc.gov/prohibited-employment-policiespractices) (HR-S3). Lists employment practices in which discrimination on protected bases is unlawful, from job advertisements and hiring through pay, benefits, discipline, harassment, and retaliation, and notes that neutral policies with disparate effect must be job-related and necessary to the business. Motivates the collection's boundary that no label classifies a person's protected characteristics, HR01 as separating essential from preferred wording so a person can review essential requirements, HR02 as routing of pay, benefits, and conduct questions, and HR04 as intake routing of treatment, harassment, and retaliation concerns to the organization's own process. Motivation only, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
