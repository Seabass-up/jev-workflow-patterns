# FN05 — Vendor or customer message: dispute, inquiry, remittance advice, or payment demand

[Finance catalog]({{ '/finance/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/finance/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/finance.md)

Classify what a message from a vendor or customer to the finance inbox primarily does so it reaches the right queue.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/finance/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Routes a shared finance inbox by what the sender is doing rather than by keyword, while invoice lookup, amount comparison, and case handling stay in code and with staff.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `counterparty_message`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

A message that contests an amount, a deduction, a short payment, a charge, or a rejected invoice as wrong is a dispute. ## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 5 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.84; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `FN05-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "counterparty_message": "Hello accounts team at Brindlemoor Supplies (ar@brindlemoor.example), this is Tomas at Quillfeather Roasters. Our payment run today included INV-EX-31 and INV-EX-32 in full; the bank reference is our usual QFR prefix followed by the invoice numbers. INV-EX-33 was held back: it charges freight twice, once as its own line and again inside the handling fee, which is not what we agreed, so we will not pay it until a corrected invoice arrives. Please confirm receipt of the two payments when they land."
}
```

Expected label: `dispute`.

The message both reports payments sent (remittance advice) and contests the double freight charge on INV-EX-33 as wrong; the contract's stated order selects dispute first when more than one kind applies.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code looks up the invoice and payment records, compares amounts and dates, opens a dispute case, or posts the remittance; the label only routes the message.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; unknown messages go to a person.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with the queue accounts payable and receivable staff chose for a month of inbound messages; review every remittance advice the label missed.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [Wikipedia: Remittance advice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance_advice) (FN-S4). Describes remittance advice as a payer's notice of a payment and the invoices it covers, used to match payments to invoices. Motivates FN05 as a message-intent reading and FN08 as a memo-consistency reading; encyclopedic motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.
- [Wikipedia: Accounts payable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accounts_payable) (FN-S1). Describes the accounts payable function: invoices received from vendors, matched to purchase orders and receiving records, approved, and paid, with supplier statements and approved expense reports as supporting documentation. Motivates FN01, FN02, FN05, FN06, FN07, and FN08 as readings of the documents and messages that function handles; encyclopedic motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
