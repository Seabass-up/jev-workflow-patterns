# ST02 — Passage reveals what the viewpoint character cannot know

[Storytelling catalog]({{ '/storytelling/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/storytelling/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/storytelling.md)

Check whether a passage written from one character's viewpoint includes knowledge that character does not have.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/storytelling/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Catches viewpoint slips that break the reader's trust in a limited point of view.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `viewpoint_character`
- `character_knowledge`
- `passage_text`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.


## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 3 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.8667; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `ST02-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "viewpoint_character": "Rook Haldane",
  "character_knowledge": [
    "Rook is in the barn at Haldane farm, alone, at dusk; the farmhouse is forty yards away across the yard.",
    "He can see the lit kitchen window from the barn door but cannot hear anything said or done inside the house.",
    "He knows his sister Nell received a letter this morning and has been short with him all day.",
    "He believes the letter came from the bank about the mortgage; he does not know it was sent by Amsel Ferrow.",
    "He knows Nell's habit of glancing at a door when she is hiding something."
  ],
  "passage_text": "Rook leaned on the barn door and watched the kitchen window go yellow, then Nell's shape cross it, then cross it back. She was lying about the letter — he'd have bet the mare on it — she had looked at the pantry door four times while she said it was nothing. Something thudded inside the house, dull and heavy; Da's chair going over again, most likely, or the coal scuttle. He thought about walking across the yard and asking her straight. Amsel Ferrow's letter was probably in the pantry by now, folded into the flour tin the way she hid the housekeeping money. He stayed where he was and let the dark come up over the yard."
}
```

Expected label: `viewpoint_broken`.

Every other statement is direct perception or framed inference (the glances, the thud 'most likely'), but the narration identifies the letter as Amsel Ferrow's, a fact character_knowledge says Rook does not have; the hedge 'probably' covers only where the letter is, not who sent it, and what the reader may know from other chapters does not count as this character's knowledge.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code maintains the per-character knowledge ledger from earlier chapters, marks omniscient or multi-viewpoint sections so they are not checked, and leaves every revision to the author.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

One re-check after the author updates `character_knowledge` with a fact established earlier.

## Verification recipe

Plant viewpoint slips in passages with an existing knowledge ledger and measure detection; then check false alarms on legitimate inference and free indirect style.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [Wikipedia: Narration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narration) (SS-3). Describes narrative point of view, including limited viewpoints restricted to what one character knows, and narrator reliability. Motivates SL02, SL03, SL08.
- [Aristotle, Poetics (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1974/pg1974.txt) (SS-1). Plot as the arrangement of incidents in which each event follows from what precedes it, with reversal and recognition as changes of situation. Motivates SL01 and SL06 as tests of whether a scene changes anything and whether a planted element is used.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
