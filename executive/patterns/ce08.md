# CE08 — Escalation matches the supplied escalation policy

[Executive catalog]({{ '/executive/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/executive/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/executive.md)

Classify whether an escalation request describes a situation the organization's escalation policy text names.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/executive/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Keeps escalations the policy does not cover off the executive agenda and sends numeric-threshold cases to code, without the model applying the threshold.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `escalation_request`
- `escalation_policy`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 4 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.85; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `CE08-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "escalation_request": "Requesting executive escalation. A contractor's laptop was stolen from a parked car on Monday; it held the draft Meridian launch plan and internal pricing worksheets, and IT has confirmed it contained no customer records and no personal data of any kind. Replacing the device, rotating the affected credentials, and engaging Halden Forensics (intake@haldenforensics.example) to confirm nothing was exfiltrated will require unbudgeted spend of about $62,000, which is above the escalation threshold, so this needs the executive team's attention this week. The department head has already been briefed and agrees it should go up.",
  "escalation_policy": "Matters are escalated to the executive team when any of the following applies: (1) any inquiry or notice from a regulator; (2) a security incident involving customer personal data; (3) unbudgeted spend above $50,000 in a single quarter; (4) the departure or planned departure of a named key-person executive; (5) any litigation filed against the company. All other matters are handled by the department head."
}
```

Expected label: `depends_on_numeric_limit`.

The stolen laptop is a near-miss on the customer-personal-data condition because the request states no customer or personal data was involved, and no other non-numeric condition is described, so the only condition that could apply is the unbudgeted-spend limit; the contract assigns depends_on_numeric_limit even though the request asserts the limit is exceeded, leaving the comparison to code.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code supplies the policy text at its current revision, compares amounts, dates, and counts against the policy's limits for depends_on_numeric_limit, routes matches to the executive queue and non-matches to the department head, and records the policy revision used.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

One re-check after the requester adds the missing detail when the label is unknown.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with which escalations the executive team accepted as within policy over a quarter; review every no_policy_condition_applies label on an accepted escalation first.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [Wikipedia: Management by exception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_by_exception) (CE-S5). Describes a management style in which routine matters are handled at lower levels and only cases that deviate from the norm or exceed set limits are brought to management's attention. Motivates CE08 as a check of an escalation request against the organization's stated escalation conditions, with any numeric limit compared in code. This is motivation for the question, not evidence of Jev performance.
- [Wikipedia: Memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorandum) (CE-S1). Describes memos and briefing notes as documents that bring a problem to notice, are marked either for information or for decision, and typically give background, identify options, and advocate a recommended one. Motivates CE01 and CE05 as readings of whether a memo states its decision and what kind of response it asks for. This is motivation for the questions, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
