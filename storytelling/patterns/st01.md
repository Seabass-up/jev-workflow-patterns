# ST01 — Scene changes the situation

[Storytelling catalog]({{ '/storytelling/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/storytelling/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/storytelling.md)

Check whether a scene leaves the story in a different situation than it started.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/storytelling/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Helps an author or editing assistant find scenes that could be cut or merged because nothing changes in them.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `scene_text`
- `situation_before`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 3 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.8667; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `ST01-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "scene_text": "Odalys walked the sea wall at dusk with the wind pulling at her coat, past the warden's hut with its new lock and the tide-board nobody had updated since Halloran died. She told herself the argument for staying: the wage, the hut, the boats she knew by the sound of their engines. Then she told herself the argument for going: Brann's face at the supper table, the farm with its stone walls, a bed that did not smell of kelp. She got as far as the harbor mouth, turned, and got as far as the hut again, and each time the case was won by whichever side she had argued last. When she came in, Brann looked up from the lamp he was mending. 'Well?' 'Not yet,' she said. 'By the end of the week, like I told them.' He nodded and went back to the lamp, and she hung her coat where it always went.",
  "situation_before": "Odalys has been offered the post of harbor warden by the Saltmarrow town council and has not given an answer; the council expects one by the end of the week. Her husband Brann wants her to refuse so they can move inland to his family's farm, and she has told him she is still thinking about it. Neither of them has spoken to the council since the offer."
}
```

Expected label: `situation_unchanged`.

The scene is vivid and full of deliberation, but by its end no decision is made, nobody learns anything new, the relationship, obstacle, and deadline stand exactly as in situation_before; the contract classes conversation and description that end with the same knowledge, decisions, and goals as no change, however vivid.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code supplies the scene boundaries and the author's own summary of the situation before it, keeps the author's decision about cutting or keeping a scene, and never edits text.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

One re-check after the author revises `situation_before` when it omitted something the scene relies on.

## Verification recipe

Have the author mark scenes they consider static in a manuscript; compare with the label and record which static scenes the author still wants for tone.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [Aristotle, Poetics (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/1974/pg1974.txt) (SS-1). Plot as the arrangement of incidents in which each event follows from what precedes it, with reversal and recognition as changes of situation. Motivates SL01 and SL06 as tests of whether a scene changes anything and whether a planted element is used.
- [Wikipedia: Story structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrative_structure) (SS-2). Summarizes structural frameworks in which scenes advance a causal chain toward resolution. Motivates SL01 and SL08; an encyclopedic overview, not craft authority.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
