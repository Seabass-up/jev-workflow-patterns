# RE02 — What a property inquiry asks for

[Real estate catalog]({{ '/real-estate/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/real-estate/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/real-estate.md)

Classify what a message about a listed property asks the brokerage or leasing office for.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/real-estate/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

<div class="callout warning"><p><strong>Provisional.</strong> At least one screening case disagreed with its authored label or did not complete. The disagreement is preserved on the evaluation page; neither the contract nor the label was changed after inference.</p></div>

## Proposed benefit

Routes inquiries to the right desk by what the message actually asks instead of by keyword, while the listing system and a person supply every answer.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `inquiry_message`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 5 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.84; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `RE02-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "inquiry_message": "Hello, I saw the listing for the one-bedroom at 14 Larkspur Court, unit 2, on your site. I don't need a tour; I've been inside that building before. Is the rent at all negotiable, and is the unit still available for a November 1 move-in? Does it come with an assigned parking space?"
}
```

Expected label: `availability_question`.

The tour is declined rather than requested, so showing_request does not apply. The message then asks about availability, price, and a feature, and the contract's precedence puts availability_question ahead of pricing_question and property_details_question regardless of sentence order.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code routes the message to the listing agent or leasing office, records the listing identifier, and never answers a pricing or availability question from the label; the listing system or a person supplies the answer, and routing does not vary by anything about the sender.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; unknown messages go to the office inbox for a person to read.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with how the leasing office categorized a month of inquiries; review messages where the label chose a different first request than the office did.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [HUD: Housing Discrimination Under the Fair Housing Act](https://www.hud.gov/helping-americans/fair-housing-act-overview) (RE-S1). HUD's overview states that housing discrimination is illegal in nearly all housing, that the Fair Housing Act protects people from discrimination when renting or buying a home, getting a mortgage, seeking housing assistance, or engaging in other housing-related activities, and lists the protected classes: race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, and disability. Motivates the boundary applied across RE01, RE02, RE05, and RE08: every label reads text about a property, a document, or a request and never judges or infers anything about a person. This is motivation for the design, not legal advice.
- [Wikipedia: Real estate contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_contract) (RE-S5). Describes purchase contracts for real property, including identification of the property and its fixtures, and contingencies such as financing, inspection, appraisal, and the sale of another property that condition the buyer's obligation. Motivates RE07 (contingency language), RE01 (a listing claim checked against the property record), and the subjects of RE02 inquiries; no contract term is interpreted by the model. Motivation for the design, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
