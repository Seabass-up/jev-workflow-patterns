# CN02 — Cause a daily log gives for lost time

[Construction catalog]({{ '/construction/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/construction/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/construction.md)

Classify which cause a daily log entry states for time lost or work held, so weather, material, labor, and information days can be counted separately.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/construction/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Turns free-text daily logs into countable delay causes without a person re-reading a month of entries, while the contract decides which delays are excusable.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `log_entry`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `CN02-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "log_entry": "Cedar Ridge Clinic daily log, superintendent D. Okafor, Thursday. Wind gusts all afternoon stayed within the crane's posted limits, so steel picks continued and no weather time was lost. The rebar delivery for the east footings never arrived; the supplier's dispatcher confirmed the truck was sent to their other yard by mistake. The four ironworkers shifted to formwork on the west line and the east footing pour is held until the steel is on site. On site: six laborers, four ironworkers, two carpenters, one operator."
}
```

Expected label: `materials_or_deliveries`.

The entry attributes the held pour to a delivery that did not arrive, and it expressly states the wind lost no time, so weather is not a stated cause and the precedence order never reaches it; the crew was present and reassigned, so no labor cause is stated either.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code owns the day count per cause, the calendar and working-day rules, notice deadlines, and whether a delay is excusable or compensable under the contract; the label records the stated cause only.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; an unknown label sends the entry back to the superintendent for a one-line clarification.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with the superintendent's own delay coding for a month of logs and with the causes accepted in later time-extension requests; review every entry labeled no_delay_stated that a later claim relied on.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [Wikipedia: Construction management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_management) (CN-S5). Describes construction management as integrating cost, schedule, quality, safety, and scope, lists quality and safety management, contract administration, and documentation and claims management among its responsibilities, states that project documentation includes diaries, logs, and daily field reports kept as records for dispute resolution, and names the warranty period and closeout as the final phase. Motivates CN02 (daily logs), CN05 (safety observations), CN06 (punch lists as a quality and closeout record), and CN08 (closeout documents); motivation only, not evidence of Jev performance.
- [Wikipedia: Critical path method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_path_method) (CN-S4). Explains that critical activities have no float and cannot be delayed without lengthening the project, that non-critical activities carry float, that the technique is widely used to plan and control construction projects, and that as-built critical path analysis is used to assess the causes of a delay. Motivates CN07 as a reading of whether a note states a milestone or completion impact versus float absorption, and CN02's separation of stated delay causes; all float and date computation stays in the scheduling software. Motivation only, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
