# GR05 — Response shows its working or only a final answer

[Grading catalog]({{ '/grading/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/grading/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/grading.md)

Classify whether a math or science response shows the steps that lead to its answer, gives only a final answer, shows steps without an answer, or gives neither.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/grading/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

<div class="callout warning"><p><strong>Provisional.</strong> At least one screening case disagreed with its authored label or did not complete. The disagreement is preserved on the evaluation page; neither the contract nor the label was changed after inference.</p></div>

## Proposed benefit

Separates responses the teacher can follow from bare answers before marks are assigned; correctness is checked by the teacher or code.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `problem_statement`
- `student_response`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 5 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.84; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `GR05-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "problem_statement": "Solve for x: 3x + 4 = 25.",
  "student_response": "x = 7. Check: 3 times 7 is 21, and 21 + 4 = 25, which matches the right-hand side, so the answer works."
}
```

Expected label: `working_shown`.

The response states a final answer and also contains equations with intermediate results (21, then 25) that go beyond a restatement of the problem, so it cannot be final_answer_only. The contract says a response with some steps and a stated final answer is working_shown even if the steps are incomplete, and it does not require the steps to precede the answer or to derive rather than verify it.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code compares the stated answer with the answer key exactly, applies the mark scheme, and records the label as a reading flag; method marks and partial credit stay with the teacher.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; final_answer_only and no_working_no_answer labels list the item for the teacher.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with the teacher's method-mark decisions on a marked set; review every working_shown label where the teacher awarded no method marks.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [GOV.UK: Reducing teacher workload: Marking Policy Review Group report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reducing-teacher-workload-marking-policy-review-group-report) (GR-S2). A government review group's report on reducing the workload of marking, setting out that effective marking should be meaningful, manageable, and motivating. Motivates GR03 (whether a comment gives the student something to do) and the collection's aim of directing a teacher's reading time; also the reading-order use of GR01, GR05, and GR08. Motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.
- [Wikipedia: Rubric (academic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubric_(academic)) (GR-S1). Describes a scoring rubric as a set of criteria, each with levels of quality and a descriptor for each level. Motivates GR02 (evidence for one criterion at a time), GR07 (whether adjacent level descriptors state a boundary), and the per-criterion framing of GR01 and GR05. Motivation for the patterns, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
