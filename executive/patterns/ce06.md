# CE06 — Risk section names a downside or only upside

[Executive catalog]({{ '/executive/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/executive/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/executive.md)

Classify whether a proposal names a specific way its plan could fail, mentions risk only generically, or mentions none.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/executive/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

<div class="callout warning"><p><strong>Provisional.</strong> At least one screening case disagreed with its authored label or did not complete. The disagreement is preserved on the evaluation page; neither the contract nor the label was changed after inference.</p></div>

## Proposed benefit

Separates proposals that have thought about failure from those that say risks are manageable, without judging whether the named risk is the right one.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `proposal_text`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 4 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.85; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `CE06-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "proposal_text": "Proposal: launch the Meridian mid-market bundle in the spring cycle with a dedicated sales pod. Risks: as with any launch there is execution risk, and there are the usual uncertainties around timing and adoption, all of which we consider manageable. The real risk is in not launching. If we hold the bundle back another cycle, Corvane Industrial will lock up the mid-market accounts we are targeting, our renewal conversations in that segment will get harder, and the sales pod we have already hired will sit idle. The upside is a new revenue line and a foothold in a segment where we currently have none."
}
```

Expected label: `generic_risk_only`.

The specific harms named are consequences of not adopting the plan, not ways the recommended course could fail, cost more, take longer, or cause harm; the plan's own risks are mentioned only as generic manageable execution risk, so no specific downside of the recommended course is stated.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code returns upside_only and generic_risk_only proposals to the author with a request for specific downsides; whether a named downside is likely or mitigated is the reader's judgment.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

One re-check after the author revises the risk section.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with reviewer notes on past proposals about missing or vague risk discussion; review every downside_stated label on a proposal reviewers called vague.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.
