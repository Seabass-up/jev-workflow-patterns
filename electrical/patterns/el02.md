# EL02 — Reported condition calls for de-energizing before work

[Electrical catalog]({{ '/electrical/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/electrical/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/electrical.md)

Classify whether a described condition indicates an immediate electrical hazard that must be made safe before any other work.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/electrical/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Puts the first safety decision on a consistent footing while the licensed electrician on site retains authority.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `condition_report`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 4 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.85; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `EL02-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "condition_report": "Storm runoff flooded the finished basement last week to about knee height. The homeowner switched off the breaker for the basement receptacle circuit before the water rose, and it has stayed off; the panel is upstairs and stayed dry. Four wall receptacles on that circuit were under water for two days, the water has since been pumped out, and the homeowner wants to know whether the circuit can be turned back on. No shock, smell, smoke, or scorching has been noticed, and nothing on that circuit has been touched since."
}
```

Expected label: `needs_assessment`.

The contract's hazard sign is water contact with energized equipment, and the report states the submerged receptacles were de-energized before the water rose and remain off, so no immediate-hazard sign is described; devices that were under water still require a qualified assessment before re-energizing, so needs_assessment applies rather than routine.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code enforces the response policy for each label, records the on-site electrician's own hazard determination as authoritative, and applies lockout and de-energizing procedures independently of any model output.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; an immediate_hazard label triggers the standing response, not another question.

## Verification recipe

Review labels against incident reports and the electrician's on-site determinations; measure missed hazard signs first.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [NFPA 70E, Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace (NFPA overview page)](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70e-standard-development/70e) (ES-1). NFPA describes 70E as requirements for safe work practices that protect personnel by reducing exposure to major electrical hazards, originally developed at OSHA's request, helping avoid injuries and fatalities from shock, electrocution, arc flash, and arc blast, and assisting compliance with OSHA workplace electrical rules. Motivates EL02 as a text classification of reported hazard signs; the standard, not the label, governs practice.
- [Wikipedia: Residual-current device](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residual-current_device) (ES-3). Describes ground-fault protective devices such as GFCIs as distinct from overcurrent devices. Motivates EL07 as a device-type classification; encyclopedic overview.
- [OSHA: Electrical - Overview](https://www.osha.gov/electrical) (ES-6). OSHA's electrical overview states that electricity is a serious workplace hazard, that its standards protect employees from shock, electrocution, fires, and explosions, and that the most frequent causes of electrical injuries include contact with power lines, lack of ground-fault protection, a missing or discontinuous path to ground, equipment not used as prescribed, and improper use of extension and flexible cords. Motivates EL02 (hazard signs) and EL07 (ground-fault protection devices); the on-site electrician's determination governs.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
