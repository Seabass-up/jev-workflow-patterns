# LC01 — Clause type from the clause text

[Contracts catalog]({{ '/legal-contracts/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/legal-contracts/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/legal-contracts.md)

Classify which of five common clause types a clause's text is, so the review checklist can file it under the right heading.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/legal-contracts/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Files clauses pulled from an unfamiliar agreement under checklist headings without a person reading each one first; the lawyer still reads every clause.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `clause_text`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 6 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.8333; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `LC01-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "clause_text": "Neither party shall be liable to the other for any indirect, incidental, or consequential damages, or for lost profits, arising out of or relating to this Agreement, and each party's aggregate liability under this Agreement shall not exceed the fees paid by Harbor Analytics to Northgate Supply in the twelve months preceding the event giving rise to the claim. The foregoing exclusions and cap shall not apply to a party's obligations under Section 9 (Indemnification) or to damages arising from a party's breach of Section 7 (Confidentiality)."
}
```

Expected label: `limitation_of_liability`.

The clause chiefly caps and excludes liability; its mentions of indemnification and confidentiality only carve those obligations out of the cap and do not themselves require anyone to indemnify or keep anything secret, so the precedence order for clauses that combine types is never reached.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code owns the checklist headings, the clause number and location in the document, and the routing of unknown clauses to a person for filing; the label files a clause and never decides enforceability, risk, or acceptability.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; a clause labeled unknown is filed by a person.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with the headings a paralegal assigned on a set of already-reviewed agreements; review every clause filed under a different heading.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [Wikipedia: Indemnity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indemnity) (LC-S1). Describes an indemnity as one party's contractual obligation to compensate another for loss, usually paired with hold-harmless wording, and quotes clause examples in which a named party shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the other. Motivates LC01 (indemnity as a clause type), LC02 (which named party is bound), and LC03 (one-way drafting). Motivation for the questions, not evidence of Jev performance.
- [Wikipedia: Non-disclosure agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-disclosure_agreement) (LC-S2). Classifies confidentiality agreements as unilateral (one-way), bilateral (mutual), or multilateral, and lists typical contents: the definition of what is confidential, the exclusions from what must be kept confidential, and a duty to give the disclosing party prompt notice of compelled disclosure. Motivates LC01 (confidentiality as a clause type), LC03 (reciprocal versus one-sided), LC06 (a defined term and its uses), LC07 (notice elements), and LC08 (carve-outs from a rule). Motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.
- [Wikipedia: Exclusion clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusion_clause) (LC-S5). Describes clauses that exclude or limit a party's liability and notes that courts read such clauses strictly and against the party relying on them. Motivates LC01 (limitation of liability as a clause type) and LC08 (whether a clause states exceptions to its own rule). How any such clause is enforced is a legal question that stays with the lawyer. Motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
