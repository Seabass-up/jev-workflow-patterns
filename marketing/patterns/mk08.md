# MK08 — Review comment on creative is about strategy or execution

[Marketing catalog]({{ '/marketing/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/marketing/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/marketing.md)

Classify whether a reviewer's comment on a creative piece challenges what the piece is trying to do, asks for a change in how it is carried out, does both, or requests no change.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/marketing/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

<div class="callout warning"><p><strong>Provisional.</strong> At least one screening case disagreed with its authored label or did not complete. The disagreement is preserved on the evaluation page; neither the contract nor the label was changed after inference.</p></div>

## Proposed benefit

Routes strategy comments to the campaign owner and execution comments to the creative team without a person triaging every thread, while every decision about the work stays with those people.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `creative_summary`
- `review_comment`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Policy recorded with its option count

Review when confidence is below 0.8. With 5 options, that floor is equivalent to a top probability of 0.84; a threshold is not portable to a contract with a different option count. These values are provisional workflow policy, not calibration.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `MK08-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "creative_summary": "Landing-page hero for the Wardenly security-monitoring launch: headline 'Built for enterprise security teams', a subhead about continuous monitoring, and a wide photo of a data-centre aisle. The brief targets engineering leads at startups with fewer than fifty employees.",
  "review_comment": "Please cut 'built for enterprise security teams' from the headline and replace the data-centre photo with something smaller-scale, like a laptop on a kitchen table. Same reason for both: we agreed at kickoff that this campaign is talking to ten-person startups, and right now it reads like it is pitching a bank's CISO."
}
```

Expected label: `strategy`.

Both requested edits are wording and imagery changes, but their only stated reason is that the piece addresses the wrong audience, and the contract counts a requested change whose stated reason is audience or positioning as a strategy comment. No separate execution point with a craft, clarity, correctness, or brand-rule reason is raised, so both does not apply.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code supplies one comment at a time with the creative summary from the review tool, routes strategy and both to the campaign owner and execution to the creative team, keeps the reviewer's wording unchanged, and never resolves, closes, or ranks a comment.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; unknown comments go to the campaign owner.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with how the campaign owner triaged a quarter of review threads; review every comment the label called execution that the owner treated as a strategy question.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [Wikipedia: Creative brief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_brief) (MK-S4). Describes the creative brief as the document that sets out a campaign's objective, target audience, key message, and how results will be judged before creative work begins, and as the reference point reviewers use when evaluating the resulting work. Motivates MK07 as a presence check for goal, audience, and metric, MK04 as a reading of copy against the brief's stated audience, and MK08 as a reading of whether a review comment challenges the brief's intent or the piece's execution. Motivation for the design, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
