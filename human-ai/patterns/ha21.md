# HA21 — Does the success metric measure the user's outcome?

[Human + AI catalog]({{ '/human-ai/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/human-ai/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/human-ai.md)

Distinguish an outcome measure from an activity proxy or an incentive that works against the explicit user goal.

**Provisional:** the initial synthetic screen includes an unresolved label mismatch for this pattern. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/human-ai/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Keep assistants optimized for useful completion rather than impressive-looking counts.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed human outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic. No study with people was conducted for this catalog, and the question must not be used to infer a person's traits, ability, diagnosis, or intent.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `HA21-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the original design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "user_goal": "Help visitors actually finish the instructions they came to use, rather than merely keeping them on the help site.",
  "success_metric": "Number of help pages opened per visitor.",
  "stated_consequence": "The team earns a bonus for dividing a formerly complete answer across extra pages, even when visitors abandon the instructions before finishing."
}
```

Expected label: `conflicting_incentive`.

Although page views can be an activity proxy, the stated reward explicitly favors extra navigation despite abandonment, contrary to the completion goal.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Define measurement protocols and thresholds with the user; calculate metrics deterministically; sample real user outcomes. A semantic alignment label is not proof the metric is reliable or the experience improved.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

If proxy-only, propose one direct outcome check and retain the proxy only as a diagnostic; do not silently replace the user's success criterion.

## Verification recipe

Run a small task-based study including a deliberately high-activity/low-outcome case; verify the metric does not call that case successful.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed. Separate agreement with synthetic expected labels from successful execution of the real workflow and from any measured benefit to a person.

## Source inspiration

- [Google PAIR: User Needs + Defining Success](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/user-needs/) (HD-S2). Official design guidance distinguishes augmentation from replacing work people want to do, and warns that narrow reward functions can miss broader outcomes. These are design principles, not empirical Jev performance claims.
- [Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics (Bastani et al., PNAS 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) (HD-S7). An original high-school mathematics study distinguished assisted practice performance from later unaided learning; teacher-designed hint safeguards mitigated negative effects in that setting. This does not establish general tutoring effectiveness or Jev efficacy.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
