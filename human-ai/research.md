# A bounded deep research pass

The aim was to find useful new applications, not inflate the catalog with renamed relevance checks. Three parallel passes examined learning and clear instructions, everyday service communication, and human–AI agency. Together they retained 21 primary sources and 24 candidates.

The previous 180 profile titles and nearest contracts were used for local overlap checks. Each new page records the closest prior patterns and its narrower distinction. This is not an exhaustive literature review, search of every public Jev project, or claim of worldwide novelty.

## Jev's role

Jev was used early for source-relevance ranking, research-need alignment, and a bounded comparison of candidate and existing-pattern summaries. Six research attempts are retained separately: five successes and one HTTP 529. The five successes include a single bounded recovery of that error. One early ranking is preserved only as observed metadata and answers, so it is not advertised as a complete request replay.

The learning pass's source ranks were mainly background relevance. The retrieval-exercise adaptation had lower source-alignment confidence (0.62) than its peers. These observations remain visible; relevance judgments are not evidence of effectiveness. Host review retained sources, verified scope, and authored the final contracts.

The 100 fixture-screening attempts are another evidence track. [Read their results]({{ '/human-ai/evaluation/' | relative_url }}). Only the supplied synthetic text was sent for these tests.

## Review before inference

A separate agent received each contract without the author's original examples or any fixture results, then authored one nonempty boundary case. Integration review clarified nine contracts before freezing: person-versus-work praise; mixed correct/incorrect mechanisms; cross-stage requirement conflicts; mixed re-asking; mixed recovery guidance; cross-dimensional feedback scope; mixed capability promises; compound observations; and conflicting-incentive precedence.

The challenge labels were retained as authored. No inference output was used to choose them. All 24 contracts and 96 labels were then frozen before testing. The challenge writers remain agents in the same workflow, not independent human raters.

## What was deliberately left out

Do not infer learner ability, disability, personality, mental health, emotion, or hidden intent. Do not transform feedback into persistent memory or preferences without explicit authority. Do not use an advisory label as grading, accessibility certification, verified task completion, or action permission. Exact arithmetic, required-field validation, identity binding, dates, source access, and consequential verification belong to code and authorized reviewers.

Statements in those dated stage notes that fixtures were not yet run describe that earlier phase; the evaluation page is the completed screening status.
