# GR08 — Section labelled conclusion actually concludes

[Grading catalog]({{ '/grading/' | relative_url }}) · [Evaluation and limitations]({{ '/grading/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) · [Kernel reference module](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Seabass-up/jev-workflow-patterns/main/skills/jev-question-kernel-catalog/references/grading.md)

Classify whether an essay's conclusion section draws the body together, only restates it, or introduces material the body did not develop.

This is a proposed, bounded text judgment. Consult the [evaluation record]({{ '/grading/evaluation/' | relative_url }}) for observed results; the fixture labels below are intended outputs.

## Proposed benefit

Points the teacher to conclusions that restate or wander before the essay is graded; the grade and the feedback wording stay with the teacher.

This is a proposed benefit, not an observed outcome. All fixtures on this page are synthetic.

## Required named state

Supply the following named fields as the evidence packet:

- `essay_body`
- `conclusion_section`

Code deterministically returns `unknown` without invoking Jev when any required named field is absent or empty; the model is not the required-field gate. The live missing-input case below probes the contract wording, not that gate.

## Complete synthetic example

This is fixture `GR08-4`, from the **challenge** split. Its author received the question contract without the design fixtures or model results; the expected label is an authored interpretation of the contract, not a Jev prediction.

```json
{
  "essay_body": "In the novel The Weir at Callenmoor, the town's decline begins with the dam. The opening chapters show the dam slowing the river until silt settles across the channel. The middle chapters show the silted river choking the harbour, so that the fishing boats can no longer reach open water. The final chapters show the harbour's failure emptying the town, as families follow the boats to the coast. My thesis is that the novel presents the town's decline as a single chain of consequences that starts with the dam.",
  "conclusion_section": "In the end, then, the dam changed the river, the river changed the harbour, and the harbour changed the town. The novel shows the decline as one chain of consequences, and that chain begins at the dam."
}
```

Expected label: `restates_only`.

The conclusion sounds like a synthesis, but its chain sentence lists the three body points in lightly changed words and its second sentence repeats the thesis, adding no closing claim, answer, implication, or consequence the body did not already state. No new argument, evidence, or topic is raised, so the contract's restates_only criterion applies.

## Code-owned responsibilities

Code locates the section the student labelled as the conclusion, supplies the body unchanged, and records the label for the teacher's reading; what the conclusion earns and how the feedback is worded stay with the teacher.

The judgment is advisory. Code and the responsible person retain control of exact identifiers, data access, state changes, and consequential verification.

## Bounded follow-up

None; restates_only and introduces_new_material labels are listed for the teacher.

## Verification recipe

Compare labels with the teacher's comments on conclusions across a graded set; review every concludes label where the teacher commented that the conclusion merely restated.

This describes required verification work, not checks already performed.

## Source inspiration

- [UNC Writing Center: Conclusions](https://writingcenter.unc.edu/tips-and-tools/conclusions/) (GR-S5). A university writing center handout on what a conclusion should do for a reader, with strategies to avoid such as ending on a rephrased thesis with nothing added or introducing a new idea or subtopic in the conclusion. Motivates GR08 (concludes, restates only, or introduces new material) and the on-topic framing of GR01. Motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.
- [GOV.UK: Reducing teacher workload: Marking Policy Review Group report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reducing-teacher-workload-marking-policy-review-group-report) (GR-S2). A government review group's report on reducing the workload of marking, setting out that effective marking should be meaningful, manageable, and motivating. Motivates GR03 (whether a comment gives the student something to do) and the collection's aim of directing a teacher's reading time; also the reading-order use of GR01, GR05, and GR08. Motivation, not evidence of Jev performance.

These sources motivate the design problem and its boundaries. They do not demonstrate Jev performance on this question.
